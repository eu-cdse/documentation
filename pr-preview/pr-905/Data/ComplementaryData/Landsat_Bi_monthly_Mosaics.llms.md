# Landsat Bi-monthly Mosaics

The Landsat Bi-monthly Mosaics dataset represents a global, cloud-free, and reconstructed historical Landsat spectral bands along with a clear sky mask at a spatial resolution of 30 meters and temporal resolution of bimonthly intervals from 1997 onwards. The dataset was generated by OpenGeoHub using the GLAD Landsat ARD version 2 as the primary input for temporal aggregation and the imputation of missing values. As a result the product delivers complete, gap-filled time series of surface reflectance. This is one of the major outputs of the [Open-Earth-Monitor Cyberinfrastructure](https://EarthMonitor.org) (OEMC) project. The OEMC project has received funding from the European Union’s Horizon Europe research and innovation programme under grant agreement No. 101059548.

The dataset reduces the original 1.4PB GLAD Landsat ARD collection to approximately 100TB by removing contaminated part of images, performing weighted temporal aggregation, and applying SWA (Seasonally Weighted Average) for reconstructing the times-series associated with each pixel. All data are stored as 8-bit Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs (COG) with deflate compression applied to chunks of 1,024 × 1,024 pixels.

## Input Data Source

The dataset is built upon the GLAD Landsat ARD version 2, which harmonizes imagery from three satellite missions: Landsat 5 TM, Landsat 7 ETM+, and Landsat 8 OLI/TIRS. The original product consists of 16-day composites (https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12030426).

## Processing Workflow

The production workflow transforms the raw GLAD Landsat ARD through three main processing stages. First, cloud screening is applied using the GLAD quality assessment flags to identify and mask contaminated pixels, including clouds, cloud shadows, haze, and related proximity artifacts, as well as other atmospheric contaminants and sensor artifacts. This creates a space-time mask that identifies clear-sky observations suitable for further processing.

The second stage performs weighted temporal aggregation to create bimonthly composites. Four consecutive 16-day images are combined into single bimonthly time frames using weighted averaging, where each pixel is weighted by the clear-sky fraction of its associated image. This approach prioritizes images with lower cloud cover, minimizing cloud-induced artifacts in the aggregated product. Bi-monthly aggregated product is stored as 8-bit unsigned integers with values ranging from 0 to 250, with 255 reserved for no-data values, and requires approximately 60TB of storage after compression.

The final stage applies time-series reconstruction using the Seasonally Weighted Average (SWA) method to fill remaining data gaps (about 40.1% of the pixels are no data). This technique leverages the annual seasonality patterns inherent in most land surface reflectance data, giving higher weight to images from the same season in different years while also prioritizing more recent observations to track land cover changes. The method uses a causal approach, meaning it only uses past images for reconstruction, which enables consistent updates when new data becomes available. The independent accuracy assessment indicates a varying R-square between reconstructed and out-of-bag hold-out pixels between 0.79 (NIR) and 0.96 (SWIR2). Many artifacts and issues are still present, even visually visible, especially for winter months (December to March) in the Northern hemisphere and in tropics (dominated with clouds), so users are advised to use the winter months images with care.

## Available spectral bands and clear sky mask

The reconstructed product includes all seven spectral bands from the original Landsat data: blue, green, red, near infrared (NIR), short-wave infrared 1 (SWIR1), short-wave infrared 2 (SWIR2), and thermal. Each band is stored separately with the same temporal resolution and spatial extent, allowing users to access only the specific bands needed for their applications. Accompanying the reconstructed spectral bands is a clear sky mask.

## Updates and improvements

Users are advised to submit issues and improvement requests directly via [Github](https://github.com/openlandmap/scikit-map/). An improved monthly version of the Landsat complete (cloud-free) consistent mosaics (V2) is planned for release at the [OEMC Global workshop](https://earthmonitor.org/global-workshop-2026/) in Barcelona 7–9 October 2026.
